## Abstract

Role engagement, the extent to which people bring themselves into the roles they occupy in social interaction, is increasingly scarce as people are often physically present but cognitively and emotionally elsewhere. Prior research implies that authenticity and deeper engagement are related, but taking a variance-based approach, this work only explores how authenticity may affect role engagement, and assumes authenticity derives from alignment between a role and one’s real self. In this paper, we ask how the experience of authenticity and role engagement coevolve in social interaction. Drawing on interviews, observations, and text analysis of live-action role-playing games (Jubensha) in China, we develop a four-stage process model of the coevolution of authenticity and role engagement. We show that authenticity and engagement develop in a back-and-forth manner, and that deep social, emotional and cognitive engagement in a prescribed role can itself produce the experience of authentic self-expression, even when the role is unreal. These findings challenge the view that authenticity stems from alignment and realness, and open the black box of the social processes linking authenticity and role engagement.
