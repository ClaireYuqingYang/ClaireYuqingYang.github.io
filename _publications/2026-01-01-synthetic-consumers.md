## Abstract

Consumer researchers increasingly use AI agents to simulate behavior, but where the language model belongs remains unclear. I use a benchmark pairing 1,003 people with LLM “digital twins” built from their survey responses. The LLM twins reproduce the population-level experimental null but fail to predict their matched humans. I then embed the same agents in a misinformation-diffusion network and impose four known-direction counterfactual shifts. When the LLM only supplies perceptions and an explicit mechanism governs diffusion, the simulator recovers all four directions; when the LLM generates behavioral responses directly, it detects only two, one of them reversed.
